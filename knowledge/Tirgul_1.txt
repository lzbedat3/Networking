In simple words: the maximum amount of data that
can be transferred per second on a link
Measured in bits per second:
bps · Mbps )Mega bits per second( · Gbps (Giga bits per

 Example

Things we will learn in a bit ;)
1. Bandwidth (Transmission Rate): the
 capacity of the line, the number of bits
 processed per unit of time. Measured in bp/s
 (bits per second), kb/s, mb/s, etc.
2. Throughput: the actual data transferred over
 a certain amount of time. In most cases it is
 less than the bandwidth.
3. Packet transmission delay: the time required
 to push all the packet's bits into the wire, i.e.,
 the delay caused by the data-rate of the link.
Units: amount of data
Bits are the units used to describe an amount of data in a
network
1 kilobit (Kbit) ≈ 1 x 103 bits = 1,000 bits
1 megabit (Mbit) ≈ 1 x 106 bits = 1,000,000 bits
1 gigabit (Gbit) ≈ 1 x 109 bits = 1,000,000,000 bits
Bytes describe a series of eight bits: 1 Byte = 8 bits
 Watch the letter
 b = bit, B = Byte. 100 Mbps is not the same as 100 MBps

 Q: How long until the caravan is lined up before the
 2nd toll booth?
Caravan analogy: the answer
• Time to “push” the entire caravan through the toll booth onto
 the highway = 12 x 10 = 120 sec
• Time for the last car to propagate from the 1st to the 2nd toll
 booth: 100 km / (100 km/hr) = 1 hr
 A: 62 minutes (2 min + 60 min)
Caravan analogy (more)
• suppose cars now “propagate” at 1000 km/hr
• and suppose the toll booth now takes one min to service a car
 Q: Will cars arrive at the 2nd booth before all cars
 are serviced at the first booth?
Caravan (more): the answer
 A: Yes! After 7 min, the first car arrives at the second
 booth; three cars are still at the first booth
Why?
• The first car leaves the booth after 1 min, then drives 100 km at 1000
 km/hr = 6 min → arrives at minute 7
• By minute 7 the booth has serviced 7 cars, so 3 are still waiting
• In a network: the first bits of a packet can reach the router before
 the whole packet has been transmitted

Calculating delay, step by step
Transmission time
 Transmission time = packet length / channel capacity
How long does it take A to transmit an entire packet on
the link?
 Relevant information
 packet length = 1500 Bytes
 channel capacity = 100 Mbps
Another way to ask this question:
If the link can transmit 100 million bits in a second, how many
seconds does it take to transmit 1500 Bytes (8 x 1500 bits)?
Transmission time: solution
 Convert Bytes to bits
 1500 Bytes x 8 = 12,000 bits
 Divide by the capacity
 t = 12,000 bits / 100,000,000 bps
 Result
 t = 0.00012 sec (or 120 µsec)
 t = (1500 x 8 bits) / (100 Mbps) = 120 µsec
Propagation delay
 Propagation delay = distance / propagation speed
How long does it take a single bit to travel on the link
from A to B?
Relevant information: link distance = 1000 m
